Vietnam's tourism industry just hit a turning point. Not recovery. Not gradual rebuilding. **Acceleration.**

The numbers tell the story. **10.6 million international visitors** landed in Vietnam between January and May 2026—the highest January-to-May result on record. That's 42 percent of the country's aggressive **25 million annual target**. And it's happening because Vietnam isn't just promoting tourism anymore. It's fundamentally rewiring how tourists move through the country: digitally, sustainably, and at scale.

What started as a pandemic survival story has become a strategic blueprint. Vietnam is betting that smarter booking systems, e-visa automation, airport tech upgrades, and structured trade events can turn the world's travel momentum into sustained, profitable growth. For airlines, hotel operators, DMCs, and destination marketers, that shift matters enormously.

## The Momentum Behind Vietnam's Tourism Resurgence

Let's be clear about where Vietnam stands. In 2025, the country welcomed **nearly 21.2 million international visitors**—that's **20.4 percent growth year-over-year** and **17.8 percent higher than pre-pandemic 2019 levels**. The recovery didn't just happen. It crushed the baseline.

May 2026 numbers show the streak continuing. Around **1.8 million international arrivals** in a single month outside the peak winter season proves demand isn't seasonal noise. It's structural.

But here's the critical insight: growth alone won't sustain Vietnam's ambitions. Global tourism in 2026 is expanding unevenly. Travellers are more price-conscious. Airlines face capacity pressure. Destinations need data, frictionless booking, and superior service quality to convert interest into actual sales.

Vietnam's leadership understands this. Rather than rely on organic demand, the country is layering digital infrastructure, airport automation, and targeted trade events on top of rising visitor numbers. The result is a flywheel effect—demand energizes digital investment, which removes friction, which attracts more visitors, which justifies more infrastructure.

## VITM 2026: Where Digital Tourism Becomes Business

The **Viet Nam International Travel Mart 2026** is not just another trade show. It's the visible proof point of Vietnam's industry-wide transformation strategy.

Picture this: **450 exhibition booths**, **80,000 expected visitors**, and a structured B2B programme bringing together **350 to 400 international and domestic buyers**. That's approximately **150 international travel companies** and **200 Vietnamese suppliers** meeting in one place with one mission: rebuild Vietnam's tour product for 2026 demand patterns.

For tour operators, this is where smarter itineraries get sold. For airlines, it's where seat allocations and routing agreements happen. For hotels and DMCs, it's direct access to the buyers who move the volume. The themed focus on "digital transformation and green growth" signals that Vietnam isn't interested in nostalgia tourism or commodity pricing. The MICE (meetings, incentives, conferences, events) angle is particularly sharp. Group business requires proof of infrastructure, professional handling, and destination sophistication. ## Airport Expansion: Physical Backbone for Digital Growth

You cannot run smart tourism infrastructure without physical capacity. Vietnam is expanding aggressively on that front.

**Long Thanh International Airport** is the flagship project. The Airports Corporation of Vietnam (ACV) launched a **180-day acceleration campaign** to ready Long Thanh for operations in 2026. This isn't incremental. It's a new gateway designed to absorb long-haul growth, reduce bottlenecks at existing airports, and position Vietnam as a regional hub rather than just a destination.

**Noi Bai International Airport** (Hanoi's primary international gateway) already delivered results. **Terminal 2 expansion lifted capacity from 10 million to 15 million passengers annually**. Post-expansion, the airport now handles **more than 50,000 international passengers daily**, with integration of automation and digital processing systems.

These upgrades directly support tourism targets: faster baggage handling, automated passenger processing, better airline operations during peak periods, reduced bottlenecks on arrival, and smoother integration with downtown transport.

## E-Visa: The Frictionless Entry Gateway

Here's where Vietnam's digital strategy gets tangible for individual travellers.

The **official e-visa portal** lets eligible foreign nationals apply online for visas valid up to **90 days**, with options for single or multiple entry. Processing takes **three working days**. Cost: **USD25 for single-entry, USD50 for multiple-entry**.

For travel agents and tour operators, this clarity is gold. Visa friction kills conversion. Clear, transparent, fast e-visa processing removes one major objection in the booking chain. It opens Vietnam to last-minute planners, regional hoppers (Vietnam + Cambodia + Thailand circuits), and multi-month bleisure travellers.

The e-visa system is part of a larger strategic play. As Vietnam digitizes its entry process, it becomes exponentially easier for DMCs and operators to bundle Vietnam with neighbouring ASEAN destinations. A regional ticketing and visa automation platform becomes possible. That's why e-visa clarity matters beyond just visa processing—it's foundational to Vietnam's positioning as a regional hub.

## 5G and Smart Tourism Infrastructure: The Hidden Lever

Vietnam's tourism transformation doesn't exist in isolation. It's embedded within a broader **national digital infrastructure agenda targeting 5G deployment across cities, provinces, stations, ports, and international airports**.

This matters because tourism has become a live data economy. Travellers now expect mobile search, real-time maps, instant digital payments, translation apps, ride-hailing integration, online reviews, airline apps, and itinerary tracking. Destinations that can deliver seamless digital experiences convert browsers into bookings. Those that can't lose them to competitors offering friction-free travel.

Vietnam is making the infrastructure bet that supports this. By targeting 5G across transportation hubs and tourism zones, the country is creating the digital backbone that makes smart tourism platforms actually work—not in theory, but in the hands of real travellers navigating real cities.
