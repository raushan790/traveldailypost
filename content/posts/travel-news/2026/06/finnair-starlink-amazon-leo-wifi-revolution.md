## The Satellite War Is On

The race for airborne connectivity has become fierce. **British Airways**, **Lufthansa**, and **Singapore Airlines** have already made their moves, partnering with Starlink to deploy high-speed Low Earth Orbit (LEO) satellite systems across their fleets. Finnair is watching closely, knowing that Helsinki Airport's surging international traffic—up 4% in 2025 to **20.6 million passengers**—demands world-class onboard service to remain competitive as a Nordic hub.

The competition between Starlink and Amazon Leo is accelerating innovation at a pace we've never seen in aviation. Airlines that lag behind on connectivity risk losing business travelers and leisure passengers who see reliable internet as essential, not optional.

## The Real Game Changer: Speed and Reliability Over Water

Traditional satellite Wi-Fi dies over the ocean. Dead zones. Lag. Disconnections. That's the old paradigm.

LEO satellites orbit much closer to Earth than geostationary satellites, eliminating those oceanic dead zones entirely. Passengers can stream 4K video, attend video conferences, and work uninterrupted for the entire flight—whether it's a 12-hour trans-Pacific haul or a 6-hour transatlantic crossing.

For Finnair, this is transformational. Helsinki's hub connects Europe to Asia through routes that are heavily ocean-dependent. Superior Wi-Fi on those routes means Finnair can market itself as the connectivity carrier—a genuine competitive advantage.
