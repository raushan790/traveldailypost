**Singapore is about to redefine urban wellness forever.**

On a humid June afternoon in 2026, **Therme Group**—the global powerhouse behind Europe's most transformative leisure destinations—officially broke ground on something audacious: **Asia's first fully integrated wellness megacity**. At Marina South, a sprawling 720,000-square-foot complex is rising that will fundamentally reshape how millions experience health, leisure, and community in one of Asia's most densely packed cities.

This isn't just another spa resort or water park. This is social infrastructure reimagined—nine football fields worth of immersive wellbeing ecosystem, scheduled to open its gates in 2030.

## A Moment of Gravity: When Sustainability Met Ambition

The groundbreaking was led by Singapore's Minister for Sustainability and the Environment, **Grace Fu**, signaling the gravity of what's being built. This isn't a private luxury play—it's infrastructure backed by the nation's long-term urban wellness vision.

**Robert Hanea**, Founder and CEO of Therme Group, didn't mince words: "Therme Group's vision is to create a new form of social infrastructure, where wellbeing is accessible, joyful and integrated into everyday city life." His ambition is transparent—use Singapore as the flagship launchpad for replicating this model across Asia and beyond.

What makes this different from competitor projects? Therme isn't building a destination for the wealthy elite. They're designing for families with young children, burnt-out professionals, elderly residents seeking restoration, and everyone between. That's the real disruption.

## The Physical Footprint: Seven Stories of Immersion

The structure will tower across seven floors plus a basement level—a vertically engineered ecosystem that seamlessly fuses indoor and outdoor environments. The raw scale is staggering:

- **20+ pools** and water-based environments
- **70+ dedicated wellness and treatment rooms**
- **86,000+ square feet** of food and beverage spaces
- **200,000+ plants** representing over 200 native Southeast Asian species
- A **4-hectare public coastal park** connecting to Marina Barrage

This isn't accidental design. Every element serves a purpose: leisure married to therapy, entertainment woven into restoration.

## The Three Zones of Wellbeing: A Journey Architecture

The visitor experience is deliberately structured into three distinct experiential zones—each targeting a different dimension of human wellness.

### The Play Zone: Movement, Energy, Family Connection

This is where the adrenaline lives. Eighteen water slides spanning 1.8 kilometres will create one of Asia's most extensive indoor-outdoor aquatic play systems. Interactive splash environments, dedicated children's pools, and large-scale recreational water features are engineered to encourage physical activity and shared family moments in a controlled, safe environment.

### The Relax Zone: Sensory Restoration

Here, intensity gives way to calm. Thermal pools infused with minerals, hydromassage beds, and infrared therapy stations create low-stimulation restoration zones. The design philosophy is straightforward: combat the overstimulation of modern city life with intentional sensory comfort.

### The Restore Zone: Deep Therapeutic Immersion

This is the sanctuary. Saunas, steam rooms, and specialised massage treatment areas create an environment for serious wellness work—physical recovery, mental decompression, holistic rejuvenation. Think of it as wellness architecture's most contemplative floor.

## The Ecological Statement: 200+ Species, Living Architecture

What elevates Therme Singapore beyond other mega-leisure projects is its deliberate commitment to natural integration. **200,000+ plants** representing over 200 species—dominated by tropical orchids and native Southeast Asian flora—aren't decorative afterthoughts. They're functional elements.

The vegetation strategy addresses three critical goals:

1. **Urban biodiversity enhancement**—tangibly increasing regional ecological value
2. **Microclimate regulation**—lowering building temperature stress and cooling demands
3. **Air quality improvement**—creating measurable environmental benefit at scale

The adjoining 4-hectare public coastal park will physically link Therme Singapore to Marina Barrage, transforming what could've been a private luxury bubble into a genuine public-good infrastructure project.

## The Technology Layer: Frictionless Experience Design

Luxury in 2030 isn't about marble and gold leaf—it's about removing friction. Therme Singapore's tech backbone reflects this:

- **Waterproof RFID wristbands** function as keys, payment tools, and zone access—no wallets, no cards, no friction
- **AI-powered robotic locker systems** optimise storage and eliminate waiting queues
- **Next-generation water filtration** (ozone-based systems) reduce chemical dependency while maintaining safety standards

These aren't gimmicks. They're design solutions that let guests focus entirely on wellbeing rather than logistics.

## Sustainability as Non-Negotiable Core

Therme Singapore isn't greenwashing. The sustainability architecture is embedded from foundation level:

| Sustainability Feature | Intended Impact |
|---|---|
| Solar photovoltaic installations | On-site renewable energy generation |
| Smart irrigation systems | Water usage optimization across landscapes |
| Energy-efficient building systems | Reduced operational carbon footprint |
| Ozone-based water filtration | Chemical reduction + hygiene maintenance |
| Climate-responsive landscaping | Vegetation resilience, reduced environmental stress |

The development is a collaboration between **Therme Group**, **Kajima Overseas Asia (Singapore)**, and **DP Architects** alongside Therme ARC—combining international wellness expertise, construction capability, and specialised experiential design.

## The Arrival of A New Category

Here's what's truly radical: Therme Singapore isn't a luxury hotel, a water park, or a spa. It's establishing a new category altogether—*urban wellness infrastructure*. That distinction matters because it signals a fundamental shift in how cities should plan for human flourishing.

Singapore's tourism sector has historically competed on efficiency and density. Therme reframes the conversation: What if density served wellbeing instead of just commerce?

*Singapore didn't just get a new attraction in 2030—it got a permission structure for reimagining what luxury cities should be.*
