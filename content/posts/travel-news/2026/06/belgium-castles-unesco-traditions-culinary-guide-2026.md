I arrived in Belgium expecting postcard-perfect Bruges and Brussels. What I discovered instead was a country of nearly **3,000 castles**, living UNESCO traditions, and a culinary culture that rivals anywhere in Europe. This compact nation—no train ride longer than three hours between major cities—quietly ranks among Europe's most rewarding destinations for travelers willing to venture beyond the guidebook.

Belgium isn't just a pit stop between Amsterdam and Paris. It's a deliberate, layered travel experience where medieval fortress walls rise dramatically from river valleys, where fishermen still ride horses into the North Sea to harvest shrimp, and where a simple waffle from a street vendor tastes like history itself.

## The Castle Quest: Medieval Fortresses That Define Belgian Identity

**Bouillon Castle** commands the Semois River valley like something from a fever dream. Its imposing walls and archaeological layers tell the story of feudal Belgium—raw, unromanticized, absolutely magnetic. Built in the 11th century, this fortress remains one of Belgium's most visited heritage sites, and for good reason.

Then there's **Vêves Castle**, dramatically perched and dating to the 14th century. Its towers seem almost cartoonishly perfect, the kind of structure that makes you understand why European castles captivate the global imagination. Walking its interior corridors genuinely connects you to feudal times—no theme park atmosphere, just authentic stone and shadow.

**Beloeil Castle**, nicknamed the Belgian Versailles, showcases how nobility actually lived. Its manicured gardens and noble interiors reveal taste, power, and the absurd luxury of old wealth. The contrast between these three properties—one austere and military, one dramatically gothic, one aristocratically refined—proves Belgium's architectural diversity.

## UNESCO Living Heritage: Shrimp Fishing on Horseback

At **Oostduinkerke**, on Belgium's North Sea coast, something genuinely extraordinary persists. Fishermen—there are fewer than a dozen left—still ride horses into the shallow waters to harvest shrimp using traditional nets. This isn't a performance for tourists. It's a **UNESCO-recognized intangible cultural heritage practice** that's genuinely endangered.

Witnessing this tradition requires patience and timing. Tides dictate everything. But when you watch a man navigate a horse through cold Atlantic waters, net cast with generations of technique behind it, you're not observing tourism—you're witnessing living history.

Beyond Oostduinkerke, Belgium preserves heritage through weekly markets, seasonal festivals, and community celebrations in small towns and rural villages. These aren't manufactured experiences. They're how Belgians actually engage with their culture.

## Belgian Cuisine: From Street Fries to Coastal Seafood

Let's be direct: **Belgian fries**—or *frîtes*—are objectively superior to what passes for fries elsewhere. The difference isn't mystique. It's technique, potato quality, and double-frying methodology that creates that perfect crispy-exterior, fluffy-interior texture. Street vendors serve them with creamy mayonnaise that would make French fries jealous.

But Belgium's food story runs far deeper than fried potato sticks.

**Brussels** anchors the country's fine dining scene, with high-end restaurants and chocolate shops that attract culinary pilgrims. **Bruges and Antwerp** offer elegant dining experiences that balance tradition with contemporary technique. **Ghent** highlights regional specialties like **waterzooi**—a rich, creamy stew that epitomizes Flemish comfort food.

**Liège**, inland and historic, specializes in coffee culture, meatballs (*boulettes*), and waffles that are genuinely different from the tourist-trap versions. The sweetness arrives balanced, the texture crispy-tender, the ingredient list refreshingly short.

**Ostend**, the coastal jewel, serves the freshest seafood in the country. Shrimp markets operate daily. Fish restaurants along the waterfront offer catches that traveled from boat to table in hours, not days.

Culinary tours work best when you walk through local markets yourself, chat directly with vendors, and sample dishes as Belgians eat them—standing at a market stall, sitting in a neighborhood café, not in tourist-targeted restaurants. Each meal becomes cultural engagement.

## Public Transport & Walking: Belgium's Hidden Accessibility Advantage

Here's what makes Belgium genuinely brilliant for independent travelers: **navigation is straightforward**.

**Flanders** operates through **De Lijn buses and trams**. **Wallonia** uses **TEC services**. **Brussels** runs the **STIB network**. Trains efficiently connect all major cities, castles, and cultural landmarks. A single journey rarely exceeds three hours.

Walking through historic districts uncovers what guidebooks miss: hidden squares, tucked-away canal-side cafés, neighborhood architecture that reveals how Belgians actually live. The **Ardennes** region offers scenic hiking trails through forests and countryside—serious hikers can spend weeks here and barely repeat a route.

Combining public transport with walking creates full immersion while maintaining flexibility. You're not locked into guided tours or expensive car rentals. You're observing locals, joining them at sidewalk cafés, exploring markets on foot. This depth transforms tourism into genuine cultural experience.

## Why Belgium Matters as a Destination in 2026

Belgium refuses the Instagram-dominant tourism model. It doesn't need to. The country's appeal rests on genuine substance: authentic castles, living cultural traditions, food rooted in regional identity, and towns where centuries of history remain tactile and accessible.

There's no pretense here. There's no manufactured charm. Belgium offers what few European destinations still deliver—the chance to experience heritage culture as it's actually lived, not as it's been repackaged for mass consumption.

Whether you're exploring medieval fortresses, witnessing horseback shrimp fishing, sampling regional dishes at neighborhood markets, or hiking through Ardennes forests, Belgium travel promises richness that lingers long after you've left.
