## The Escape Nobody Tells You About

Summer in **New York City** hits different when the thermometer climbs into the 80s and humidity wraps around you like a wet blanket. I've watched countless travelers surrender to the heat, thinking their only beach options require advance reservations and deeper pockets than most budgets allow. They're wrong.

Here's what I discovered: six legitimate coastal sanctuaries sit within arm's reach—less than two hours by train from Penn Station. No car necessary. No Hamptons price tags. Just uncrowded shorelines, historic sites, and the kind of small-town charm that makes you question why you're ever stuck in Midtown on a summer weekend.

The Hamptons demand planning months ahead. These beaches? They're waiting for you this Saturday.

## Jones Beach State Park, Long Island
**Travel time:** Just under two hours by train  
**The pull:** Outdoor concerts, Fourth of July fireworks, and 6.5 miles of white sand

When **Robert Moses** redesigned beaches for New York City in the early 20th century, he brought **Art Deco architecture** directly to the sand. **Jones Beach State Park** is his masterpiece—a place where you can experience something entirely new each visit, according to **George Gorman Jr.**, regional director of **New York's Office of Parks, Recreation and Historic Preservation** on Long Island.

Reddit: *"Jones Beach changed my entire summer routine. Two hours from the city and zero crowds compared to the Hamptons."* — r/travel

The real draw here isn't just the coastline. **The Northwell at Jones Beach Theater** hosts top-tier performers like **James Taylor**, **Rod Stewart**, and the **Dave Matthews Band**. The adjacent **Jones Beach Bandshell** runs free summer events straight along the boardwalk, including the legendary Fourth of July fireworks and the **FourLeaf Air Show**.

Swimmers have options: designated lifeguard zones (park in field 6 if driving), calm waters at **Zach's Bay** across **Ocean Parkway**, or the two historic bathhouses. The **East Bathhouse** reopened summer 2026 after extensive restoration.

Nature lovers should hit the **Jones Beach Energy & Nature Center** for guided walks, then tackle the **WildPlay Jones Beach** obstacle course and zipline if you're feeling adventurous.

### Getting There & Eating

Take the **Long Island Rail Road (LIRR)** from **Penn Station** or **Grand Central Madison** on the **Babylon Branch** to **Freeport Station**. Downstairs, catch the **Nassau Inter-County Express northbound bus** (the **n88xpress**) to the beach. It makes two seasonal stops: **West Bathhouse** (around 17 minutes, near the boardwalk) and **Central Mall** (one minute away).

For food: grab quick bites at **Boardwalk Café** or cross the bay to **Dirty Taco + Tequila** in **Wantagh** for street-style tacos. **Salt on The Water** in **Merrick** offers dockside dining. Seven minutes away, **The Buoy Bar** serves American classics with outdoor views across **Point Lookout**.

**Where to stay:** The **Jones Beach Hotel**, opened in 1929, sits in a quiet neighborhood by **Cedar Creek Park** with vintage-style rooms and upscale Italian dining.

## Fire Island, New York
**Travel time:** One hour by train, plus 30-minute ferry  
**The pull:** Car-free island, rare maritime forest, LGBTQ+ heritage

**Fire Island** isn't just another beach getaway—it's a 32-mile barrier island that banned cars before it was trendy. Seventeen distinct communities, protected maritime forests, and seven miles of federally designated wilderness make this feel like stepping into a different era entirely.

The **Fire Island National Seashore**, established in 1969 by the National Park Service, protects the island's coastal resources and prevents overdevelopment. You can feel the difference immediately.

**Ocean Beach** serves as the unofficial capital—a singles and family-friendly hub with **Rachel's Fire Island Restaurant & Bakery** dishing out sit-down meals and famous crumb cake. Head to **Kismet** to climb the **Fire Island Lighthouse**, the state's largest, for sweeping views of the **New York City skyline**, **Atlantic Ocean**, and Long Island's bays.

The **Sunken Forest** at **Sailors Haven** is a "globally rare" maritime holly forest protected by a double dune system. It's home to tree species found nowhere else in the region. Superintendent **Alexcy Romero** with **Fire Island National Seashore** notes that a boardwalk runs through the forest, with ranger-led tours available.

**Cherry Grove** and **Fire Island Pines** ("The Pines" locally) anchor the island's deep **LGBTQ+ history**. **Cherry's On the Bay** has been drawing crowds for drag brunches for years. **The Pines** functions as a social hub with a pool club, pavilion, and **The Blue Whale**, a harborside restaurant institution.

### Getting There & Staying Put

Take the **LIRR** to one of three **Montauk Branch** stations, then shuttle or taxi to one of three ferries: **Fire Island Ferries** (western end), **Sayville Ferry Service** (central/eastern communities), or **Davis Park Ferry** (easternmost areas).

**Where to stay:** **Watch Hill** on the **Otis Pike Fire Island High Dune Wilderness** western edge works for campers. Non-campers should try **The Grove Hotel** (pool, packed events calendar) or **The Palms Hotel Fire Island** (cottage-style serenity). Vacation rentals abound.

## Long Beach, New York
**Travel time:** Under one hour by train  
**The pull:** Surfing culture, 2.1-mile historic boardwalk, summer arts scene

**Long Beach**—the "City by the Sea"—sits on **Long Island's south shore** with 3.5 miles of prime beach and serious surf credentials. Seasonal swells from southern hurricanes make it a premier spot for seasoned surfers, who paddle out from **Azores** and **Lincoln Boulevard Beach** on a rotating city schedule.

Beyond waves, explore **West End's** 1920s bungalows and boardwalk cottages. Saturday mornings bring **"Arts In The Plaza,"** an arts market running through late October.

**West Beech Street** buzzes with action: **The Merrow** (Irish pub-style dining), **Lost At Sea** (seafood and craft cocktails in an intimate space), and **Speakeasy** (craft beer institution). **Lido Kosher Delicatessen** serves comfort classics. **Riptides 11561** fries up fish and chips. **Marvel Frozen Dairy**—scooping ice cream since 1951—remains a Long Beach institution.

### Getting There & Staying Put

Take the **LIRR Long Beach Branch** from **Penn Station**, **Grand Central Madison**, or **Brooklyn's Atlantic Terminal** to **Long Beach Station**. (You may transfer at **Jamaica** depending on schedule). Purchase a 10-day or seasonal beach pass to access the sand.

**Where to stay:** The **Allegria Hotel** is **Long Island's** only oceanfront accommodation, featuring a rooftop pool with sea views and new American-style dining.

## Sandy Hook, New Jersey
**Travel time:** 50 minutes by ferry  
**The pull:** Gateway National Park, lighthouse tours, bay views

**Sandy Hook** sits as **New Jersey's** sentinel peninsula, protecting the harbor entrance with 7 miles of pristine beach and protected dunes. Part of **Gateway National Recreation Area**, this federal park operates under strict environmental guidelines, keeping the shoreline remarkably uncrowded.

The **Sandy Hook Lighthouse**, built in 1764, stands as America's oldest continuously operating lighthouse. Ranger tours climb to the observation deck for panoramic **New York Harbor** and **Atlantic Ocean** views.

Historic **Fort Hancock** tells stories of 19th-century coastal defense. The **Sandy Hook Museum** documents the area's maritime heritage, shipwrecks, and lifesaving operations.

Nature-focused visitors should explore the **Holly Forest Natural Area**—a maritime forest ecosystem rare for this region. Birdwatchers converge here during spring and fall migrations.

### Getting There & Staying Put

From **Midtown Manhattan**, take a taxi or rideshare to the **Battery Park Ferry Terminal**. The ferry to **Sandy Hook** takes 50 minutes and departs regularly year-round. Once there, the beach is immediately accessible.

**Where to stay:** **Sandy Hook** itself has no hotels—plan to day-trip or stay in nearby **Highlands** or **Sea Bright** with waterfront restaurants and small inns.

## Rockaway Beach, Queens
**Travel time:** 45 minutes by subway  
**The pull:** NYC's backyard beach, boardwalk bars, surf scene

Sometimes the best escape is hiding in plain sight. **Rockaway Beach** spans 5.5 miles of sand in **Queens**, accessible via the **A subway line** directly from **Manhattan**. No train required. No ferry. Just subway-to-sand efficiency.

The **Rockaway Boardwalk** runs the full length with bars, restaurants, and beach shacks. **Rockaway Brewing Company** serves craft beer with sand-in-your-shoes vibes. **Caracas Arepa Bar** dishes Venezuelan street food. **Café Altro Paradiso** offers oceanfront Italian dining.

The eastern end near **Rockaway Inlet** attracts serious surfers. West Rockaway toward **Fort Tilden** showcases a decommissioned military base transformed into naturalist-friendly zones and hiking trails.

### Getting There & Staying Put

Take the **A train** from **Midtown** to **Far Rockaway** or **Rockaway Park** stations (45 minutes). You're at the beach. Hotels are scarce; consider Airbnb or day-trip it.

## Coney Island, Brooklyn
**Travel time:** 40 minutes by subway  
**The pull:** Amusement park nostalgia, boardwalk culture, Nathan's Famous hot dogs

**Coney Island** isn't uncrowded, but it's undeniably authentic. Take the **D, N, or Q train** from **Midtown** (40 minutes) and you land at New York's original seaside amusement destination.

**Luna Park** operates vintage rides alongside modern thrill machines. **The Boardwalk** stretches 2.7 miles with penny arcades, game stands, and decades-old institutions. **Nathan's Famous** has been serving hot dogs since 1916—the iconic Fourth of July eating contest happens here.

The **New York Aquarium** anchors the eastern end. Year-round, it draws families and marine enthusiasts.

### Getting There & Staying Put

The **D, N, or Q train** delivers you directly. Hotels cluster along the boardwalk: **Coney Island Hotel** offers budget oceanfront rooms. Most visitors day-trip from Manhattan (weekends get crowded by noon).
