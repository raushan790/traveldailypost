The **Azamara Pursuit** carries approximately **777 passengers**—small by modern cruise standards, but perfectly sized for immersive destination experiences. The GVHA delegation toured the vessel during docking, exploring premium dining rooms, an onboard library, and the top deck's swimming pool. These aren't glitzy mega-ship features; they're refined spaces designed for travelers seeking intimate, curated experiences.

## 2026 Schedule: Victoria's Cruise Boom Begins

This is where the economics get interesting. Between **May 31 and August 26, 2026**, the **Azamara Pursuit** has **five full-day calls scheduled** for Victoria. That's five separate occasions when 777 well-heeled passengers will flood local shops, restaurants, galleries, and tour operators.

The ship operates 10- and 14-night Alaska cruises, departing from both **Whittier, Alaska** and **Vancouver, Canada**. Victoria serves as a strategic stopover—a gateway between coastal exploration and North American embarkation.

## Operational Breakthrough: Provisioning at the Terminal

Sunday's call included an operational first for Victoria: direct provisioning at the cruise terminal. Pallets of supplies—mattresses, beverages, linens, equipment—were unloaded at **Pier B** using forklifts and loaded directly onto the vessel.

This might sound mundane, but it's crucial infrastructure proof. Victoria's terminal can handle not just passenger logistics but full ship operational support. That capability signals port readiness for increased future cruise traffic, something [major ports like Vancouver have invested heavily in](https://www.portvancouver.com) over the past decade.
