No rental car. No endless driving. No regret.

That's the promise of **rail-to-trail hiking** — and right now, Europe's increasingly connected train network is making this impossibly convenient way to explore the continent's wildest landscapes more accessible than ever. Forget the logistics nightmare of traditional hiking trips. You can literally step off a train and onto a trail while most travelers are still nursing their hotel coffee.

We've tracked down eight of Europe's absolute best rail-to-trail adventures. Some take half a day. Others demand a full week. All of them put you in touch with landscapes so dramatic, you'll wonder why you ever considered driving.

## Petworth Way: Rolling Sussex Chalk and Country Villages

**Location:** Haslemere to Arundel, England  
**Duration:** 2 days  
**Train Link:** 50 minutes from London Waterloo to Haslemere

The **Petworth Way** is a masterclass in understated British beauty. This two-day meander dips straight into the **South Downs National Park**, threading through cornfields, ancient woodlands, and sleepy one-pub villages that seem frozen in time.

The poet **Alfred Tennyson** famously described the view from **Blackdown** (218m) as "green Sussex fading into blue with one grey glimpse of sea." He wasn't exaggerating. From the summit, the patchwork of purple heather, gorse, and pine sprawls endlessly toward the horizon.

Overnight in the charming market town of **Petworth** itself — home to a stunning Jacobean mansion with works by **Turner** and grounds designed by the legendary **Capability Brown**. Then push on to **Arundel**, where you'll catch an easy train connection back to London.

## Vouraikos Gorge: Greece's Hidden Ravine

**Location:** Mega Spileo to Diakopto, Greece  
**Duration:** 3-4 hours (8 miles)  
**Train Link:** Odontotos rack railway from Diakopto

Forget Greek island clichés. The **Northern Peloponnese** harbors some of Europe's most rugged, jaw-dropping terrain — and the **Vouraikos Gorge** is ground zero.

This half-day knockout follows a decommissioned railway line through a ravine so steep and cave-ridden, you'll swear you've stumbled into another world. You'll tunnel through limestone, tiptoe across rickety iron bridges, and stare up at 700-meter walls carved by millions of years of water erosion.

Start from **Zachlorou** (a one-hour walk from **Mega Spileo** station on the famous **Odontotos rack railway**). If you're feeling reverent, the clifftop monastery — founded in 362 AD by two monks from Thessaloniki — is worth a detour. The finish line is **Diakopto**, a laid-back seaside town overlooking the **Corinthian Gulf**.

From there, a connecting train whisks you to **Athens** for evening ouzo.

## Lake Bohinj: Slovenia's Mirror in the Mountains

**Location:** Bohinjska Bistrica, Slovenia  
**Duration:** 3-5 hours (7 miles)  
**Train Link:** Direct rail to Bohinjska Bistrica station

**Lake Bohinj** is what happens when Mother Nature gets everything right. A sheet of piercing blue rimmed by dense Alpine forest, this half-day circuit is sensational from the first step.

Start in the hamlet of **Ribčev Laz** (about one hour's walk from **Bohinjska Bistrica** station) and thread your way along the shoreline of **Triglav National Park**. The going is easy, the views are relentless, and the water — warming to a crisp 22°C in summer — begs for a midday swim.

In and out of woods, pebble beach to pebble beach, you'll understand why this lake sits in the heart of the **Julian Alps**. It's the kind of landscape that makes you wonder why anyone bothers with postcards.

## Caminito del Rey: Spain's Death-Defying Gorge Walk

**Location:** Ardales, Spain (Andalucia)  
**Duration:** 3-4 hours (4.8 miles)  
**Train Link:** Shuttle from El Chorro/Caminito del Rey station to trailhead

**Caminito del Rey** once held the grim title of "world's most dangerous trail." A 2014 renovation made it far safer — but didn't sacrifice an ounce of drama.

Narrow boardwalks cling to sheer limestone walls rising **700 meters** above the **Guadalhorce River**. When **King Alfonso XIII** hiked it in 1921, he didn't have the luxury of safety railings. You will.

If you can tear your eyes off the path, you'll spot **Jurassic-era fossils**, ancient juniper trees, and **griffon vultures** carving spirals through impossibly blue skies.

## Lais da Macun: Switzerland's Alpine Jewel Box

**Location:** Lavin to Zernez, Switzerland  
**Duration:** 7-8 hours (14 miles, moderately challenging)  
**Train Link:** 2 hours from Zurich to Lavin via SBB

Deep in **Graubünden**, the **Swiss National Park** guards some of the Alps' most pristine terrain. This is where the mountains get serious: glacier-capped peaks, boulder-crushing rivers, and forests that seem to predate civilization.

The crown jewel is **Lais da Macun**, a high-Alpine plateau necklaced with **23 jewel-like lakes** that shift through every imaginable shade of green and blue. This moderately challenging all-day trek from **Lavin** to **Zernez** is the finest introduction to the park.

Start early. Check the forecast obsessively. The **Swiss national railway (SBB)** runs express services from Zurich that'll deposit you in Lavin in roughly two hours. Then the mountains are yours.

## Hardangervidda: Norway's Untamed Plateau

**Location:** Finse railway station, Norway (Bergen Line)  
**Duration:** 4-5 hours (6 miles, introductory)  
**Train Link:** Finse is a major stop on the Bergen Line, one of the world's most scenic rail journeys

**Hardangervidda** is Europe's largest mountain plateau — and it is *wild*. Booming waterfalls, fjord-carved valleys, rugged peaks, and roaming **Arctic fox**, **elk**, and **wild reindeer**. This is Norway dialed to 11.

Hiking season opens as the snow melts, peaking from July to August. For a tantalizing half-day introduction, hike from **Finse** to the deeply crevassed **Hardangerjøkulen glacier** — a six-mile trek that barely scratches the surface of what this place offers.

You could walk here for days uninterrupted. Most don't. But you could. The **Bergen Line** itself — connecting Oslo to Bergen — ranks among the world's most visually stunning train journeys. Book it just for the views.

## Via Francigena: Tuscany's Medieval Pilgrimage Trail

**Location:** Lucca to Siena, Italy  
**Duration:** 7-8 days (83 miles)  
**Train Link:** ItaliaRail express services from Florence to Lucca

Walk in the footsteps of medieval pilgrims on the **Via Francigena**, one of history's most storied long-distance routes. This **83-mile section** from **Lucca** to **Siena** is pure Tuscan poetry: bell towers tolling the hours, poppies and sunflowers lighting fields on fire, **Chianti** cork-popping as the sun sinks over vine-ribbed hillsides.

You're following the ancient route of **Archbishop Sigeric the Serious**, ticking off walled medieval towns like rosary beads: **San Gimignano** (UNESCO-stamped, with 14 towers still standing), and **Monteriggioni** (mentioned by **Dante** in the Divine Comedy itself).

The complete **Via Francigena** stretches a staggering **1,383 miles** from Canterbury to Rome — taking roughly 100 days to complete. This Tuscan section is the most romantic and manageable entry point. Most hikers overnight in cozy local B&Bs rather than camping, making this a civilized pilgrimage by modern standards.

Reach Lucca via a speedy ItaliaRail connection from Florence.

## Tour du Mont Blanc: The Alpine Grand Slam

**Location:** Les Houches railway station, France  
**Duration:** 7-11 days (112 miles)  
**Train Link:** 20 minutes from Chamonix to Les Houches

This is the heavyweight champion of rail-to-trail hiking. The **Tour du Mont Blanc** circumnavigates **Mont Blanc itself** (4,808m, the Alps' tallest peak) and its signature glacier, the **Mer de Glace**, in a three-country epic across France, Italy, and Switzerland.

You'll vault over high-Alpine passes, skim thundering rivers, and cross wildflower-freckled meadows. Remote mountain refuges offer hot meals and the kind of starry nights you've forgotten exist. The beauty is off-the-charts, and the challenge is equally serious.

Hike it anticlockwise from late June through mid-September. If you want a guided experience, **Much Better Adventures** offers [10-day guided hikes](https://www.muchbetteradventures.com) that include accommodation and meals starting from **£2,144** (approximately **$2,680 USD**).

## Why Rail-to-Trail is Changing European Hiking

Europe's rail network isn't just getting you to trailheads — it's fundamentally changing how we think about hiking vacations. No pre-dawn car rental negotiations. No GPS anxiety. No highway hypnosis eating into your hiking hours.

You wake up in a city. You catch a train. You're on a trail before lunch. The logistics that once demanded weeks of planning now take minutes.

The network keeps expanding. More alpine trails get rail connections every season. More remote valleys find themselves suddenly accessible. What was once the domain of ultra-dedicated hikers now welcomes anyone with a train ticket and decent boots.

*Step off the train. Step onto the trail. Everything else is just noise.*
