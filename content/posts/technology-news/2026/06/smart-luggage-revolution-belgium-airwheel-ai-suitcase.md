Belgium just became the European flagship for what could be the most dramatic shift in airport mobility since the rollaway suitcase itself.

**Airwheel's SE3SXD AI cabin suitcase**—a 20-inch smart bag that doubles as a rideable electric scooter—launched from Brussels, signaling that **smart luggage is no longer a novelty concept**. It's becoming a regulated, scalable airport infrastructure problem.

And it's spreading fast. **China, the United States, India, the Philippines, Thailand, the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, Singapore, Malaysia, South Korea, Japan, the UAE, and South Africa** have all locked smart luggage into their travel-tech equations. But here's what matters: this isn't just a consumer gadget story. This is about how airlines, airports, and travel retailers now manage passenger mobility, lithium-battery safety, baggage tracking, and premium ancillary revenue—all at once.

## The Product That's Rewriting Airport Operations

The SE3SXD isn't just rolling luggage with a motor attached. The specifications matter intensely because they touch aviation compliance, terminal safety, and passenger assistance.

**Key product specs:**
- **Body weight:** 6.8 kg
- **Battery capacity:** 73.26 Wh removable lithium cell
- **Max riding speed:** 9.9 km/h
- **Riding range:** Up to 10 km
- **Load capacity:** Up to 95 kg
- **Battery removal time:** One second

That one-second removable battery is the commercial linchpin. Why? Because **IATA and FAA rules treat lithium batteries like controlled substances inside aircraft**. The removable design allows passengers to comply with hand-baggage battery rules while keeping the suitcase as cabin luggage—a loophole that smart-luggage makers have been hunting for years.

But there's no such thing as a universal airline acceptance. Travel sellers marketing this product need to be clear: **final approval remains at the discretion of each airline and airport security screening**. The SE3SXD's 73.26Wh battery sits comfortably below the 100Wh threshold used in aviation guidance, but that's not a guarantee of boarding approval.

## Why Lithium Battery Compliance Is the Real Gatekeeper

Here's the uncomfortable truth: **passengers don't adopt smart luggage—airlines do**.

The entire category hinges on a single regulatory question: Will the airline accept it?

[IATA guidance](https://www.iata.org) requires that removable batteries from smart baggage be carried in hand baggage if removed. Non-removable batteries may face outright rejection depending on airline policy. The [FAA's lithium-battery rules](https://www.faa.gov) explicitly state that baggage equipped with lithium batteries must be carry-on unless the batteries are removed.

Airwheel's one-second battery removal design directly addresses this bottleneck. But it's still a **B2B conversation, not a consumer promise**. Airlines control the throughput. Without airline buy-in, the smartest suitcase is just expensive luggage.

## The Terminal Bottleneck That Makes Smart Luggage Urgent

Why does this matter now? Because global airports are collapsing under passenger volume.

**Dubai, London Heathrow, and Incheon** ranked among the world's leading international hubs in 2025. **Tokyo Haneda** handled near-record total passenger traffic. These terminals aren't growing slower—they're getting more congested.

**India, Thailand, Malaysia, and the Philippines** represent the explosive growth markets. Passenger volumes are expanding faster than infrastructure can support. Smart mobility inside terminals—the ability to ride between gates, through transfer corridors, across immigration zones—becomes operationally valuable when you're moving 50,000+ people per day through a single terminal.

**Australia, New Zealand, and Canada** represent the long-haul pain point. When you're walking 45 minutes to a gate on a 15-hour flight, a motorized suitcase isn't luxury. It's utility.

The smart luggage shift isn't aspirational. It's responsive to real terminal stress.
