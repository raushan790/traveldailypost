South Korea just changed the game for domestic tourism. On **June 20, 2026**, the nation's first-ever "park golf tourism train" will depart **Seoul Station**, signaling a bold convergence of railway romance and one of Asia's fastest-growing sports. I'm talking about a complete reimagining of how travelers experience regional destinations—combining pristine countryside exploration with competitive athletic pursuits in a single, seamlessly integrated package.

**Cheongyang County** and **Korail Tourism Development** have engineered something genuinely novel here. This isn't just another train ride or another golf outing. This is a stay-type sports tourism experience designed specifically to pull metropolitan visitors into the heart of **South Chungcheong Province**, where nature, culture, and sport converge.

## The Journey Begins at Seoul Station

The itinerary kicks off with participants boarding at **Seoul Station**, then traveling to **Jochiwon Station**. From there, the real adventure unfolds. The first leg focuses on Cheongyang's cultural and natural treasures, anchored by the iconic **Chilgap Tower**—a commanding landmark that dominates the skyline.

Once at the tower, travelers get the full sensory treatment: an observation deck offering sweeping vistas, a thrilling skywalk experience, and a waterside observatory positioned to capture the region's landscape in all its glory. It's the kind of vista that reminds you why rail tourism still captivates travelers worldwide.

Nearby sits **Chilgapsan Provincial Park**, a sprawling natural sanctuary renowned for its dense forests, dramatic valleys, and deep cultural significance. Before guests even swing a golf club, they're already immersed in the region's geographic and cultural narrative.

## The Showstopper: 36 Holes at Wangjinnaru

Here's where things get serious. The crown jewel of the experience is a complete **36-hole round** at **Wangjinnaru Park Golf Course**, nestled in the scenic **Cheongnam-myeon** area. This facility was handpicked by Korail Tourism Development as the flagship venue for the train's debut—and for good reason.

The course underwent a massive expansion and infrastructure overhaul to meet national-level competition standards. The investment tells the story:

## Cheongyang's Bigger Strategic Vision

What makes this launch truly significant is that Wangjinnaru represents just one pillar of a grander vision. **Cheongyang County** is positioning itself as Korea's premier sports and tourism nexus—and the scale is ambitious.

The second pillar? An enormous **108-hole South Chungcheong Provincial Park Golf Course** currently under active development. When complete, this dual-facility strategy will create unprecedented capacity and draw sports tourists from across Asia.

The county isn't thinking small. These golf installations will be strategically linked to established regional attractions: the serene **Cheonjangho Lake**, the cultural riches of **Chilgapsan**, and the broader natural resources of South Chungcheong. The goal is explicit—revitalize the local economy through sports-driven tourism while creating a dynamic, integrated travel ecosystem.

## Accessibility Meets Ambition

One often-overlooked benefit: this train-based model makes premier golf experiences genuinely accessible to elderly tourists and sports enthusiasts who might struggle with self-driving tourism. The **Paldojangteo Tourism Train** and its dedicated connecting bus network ensure seamless transitions between attractions and the course.

This accessibility factor could be transformative. You're removing friction from the entire journey—no rental car anxiety, no navigation stress, just curated experiences on a fixed schedule.

## What This Means for Asian Sports Tourism

The launch of the park golf tourism train represents more than a single seasonal product. It's a blueprint. As Korea reinforces its identity as a [sports and wellness tourism destination](https://www.korail.com), other regions are watching.

Park golf—a gentler, more accessible variant of traditional golf—has exploded in popularity across Asia over the past decade. By bundling rail travel, cultural immersion, and sport into one cohesive offering, Cheongyang is capturing a demographic that traditional golf tourism historically missed.

The provincial and county administrations have made their commitment clear: sports welfare and regional vitality aren't afterthoughts. They're central to Cheongyang's tourism identity.

## The Paradigm Shift for Domestic Rail Tourism

When the park golf train begins operation, it will test whether travelers are ready to abandon isolated, single-activity tourism models in favor of integrated, multidimensional experiences. The evidence suggests they are.

This launch arrives as Korean domestic tourism increasingly seeks authenticity, wellness, and cultural context. A train journey that delivers athletic challenge, natural beauty, and regional heritage in a single 2-3 day package taps into exactly that demand.

The success of this inaugural season will likely trigger replications across South Korea and throughout Asia. When one region proves a concept works—that travelers will pay premium prices for integrated rail-sport-cultural packages—competition follows fast.
