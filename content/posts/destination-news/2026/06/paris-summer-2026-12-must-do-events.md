Two years have passed since **Paris** hosted the Olympic and Paralympic Games, yet the City of Lights hasn't lost its summer momentum—it's amplified it. The city has fully embraced the outdoors with swimming in the Seine, spontaneous street music, riverside dancing, and festivals that draw crowds from across the globe. If you're planning a Paris escape this summer, here are the 12 events and experiences you absolutely cannot miss.

## 1. Sunbathe on Paris Plage and Play Like a Local

The annual **Paris Plage** event returns from July through September, transforming stretches of the Seine into a vibrant urban beach. Gone are the days of imported sand and tropical palms—this year, **Paris** is leaning into Mediterranean charm with native trees, deckchairs, parasols, and open-air games.

You can picnic along the riverbanks, challenge locals to pétanque (boules) near the **Canal St-Martin**, or join in casual table tennis competitions at dedicated quayside spots. The atmosphere feels less like tourism and more like genuine Parisian summer living.

## 2. Take the Plunge: Swimming in the Seine

For decades, swimming in the **Seine** was unthinkable. But the massive cleanup effort leading up to the Olympics changed everything. Now, designated bathing spots dot the river throughout summer, and Parisians are actually embracing it.

You can swim at the **Île aux Cygnes** (with Eiffel Tower views), **Bassin de la Villette** on the **Canal de l'Ourcq**, or even in a pop-up swim section of **Canal St-Martin**. Best part? It's completely free. The catch: numbers are capped for safety, so arrive early to beat the queues.

## 3. Celebrate the Fête de la Musique on June 21

**Fête de la Musique** (World Music Day) always falls on the longest day of the year—June 21—and it's the unofficial kickoff to summer across the city. Streets, squares, bridges, and courtyards fill with live music from both professionals and passionate amateurs.

Jazz clashes with rock. Classical mingles with reggae and EDM. There's no schedule, no admission fee, just pure spontaneous joy. This is Paris at its most authentically energetic.

## 4. Experience Bastille Day Like Never Before

**Bastille Day** carries special significance in 2026. Out of respect for the victims of the 2016 terror attack in **Nice**, the traditional feux d'artifice (fireworks) and drone show will light up the sky from the **Eiffel Tower** on **July 13**—one day early.

Head to the **Trocadéro** or **Champ de Mars** for prime viewing. On **July 14**, the capital celebrates with a morning military parade along the **Avenue des Champs-Élysées**, complete with fighter aircraft and helicopter fly-bys. This year, **Ukraine** will also march in the celebrations—a historic moment.

## 5. Catch Tour de France Fever at the Finish Line

The final stage of the **Tour de France**—a grueling, 3,500km race across 21 stages—concludes on the **Avenue des Champs-Élysées** on the last Sunday of July. The energy is electric. Crowds claim spots along the barriers from dawn, waving flags and building anticipation.

If you want guaranteed views of the **Arc de Triomphe** beyond, VIP grandstand tickets are available online. When the peloton finally sweeps past in a blur of lycra and speed, the entire avenue erupts. The race is also broadcast on free-to-air TV across the city all July long.

## 6. Catch Free Open-Air Cinema at La Villette

From late July through mid-August, **La Villette** becomes an open-air cinema paradise (yes, the same spot featured in *Emily in Paris*). Bring a picnic blanket, settle onto the grass, and lose yourself in cinema under the stars.

Deckchairs can be pre-booked online for a small fee if sitting on grass isn't appealing. This year's theme is "nature," so expect films that celebrate the natural world.

## 7. Read, Relax, and Channel Your Inner Parisian in City Parks

**Paris** has no shortage of stunning green spaces perfect for an afternoon with a paperback and people-watching.

**Jardin du Luxembourg** is the most family-friendly and popular—locals and visitors alike claim spots on the famous green chairs. **Jardin des Tuileries** offers master landscape architect **André Le Nôtre's** symmetrical gardens where you can pretend to be royalty. **Jardin des Plantes** sits by the Seine with rare botanical specimens and stunning 18th-century glass greenhouses.

The **Promenade Plantée**—the world's first elevated park built atop a disused 19th-century railway viaduct—is Paris's answer to New York's Highline. Further out, **Bois de Vincennes** sprawls across 995 hectares of former hunting grounds with lakes and even a zoo.

## 8. Enjoy Free Concerts at Festivals du Parc Floral

The **Festivals du Parc Floral** runs from late June through early September within the botanical gardens of **Parc Floral de Paris**, nestled in **Bois de Vincennes**. Three distinct festivals compete for your attention:

- **Pestacles**: Family-friendly with workshops and musical shows for kids
- **Classique au Vert**: Free classical music concerts
- **Paris Jazz Festival**: Jazz performances on the open-air stage

All are completely free to attend.

## 9. Dance Salsa on the Seine Embankment

The salsa scene along the **Quai Saint-Bernard** near the **Jardin des Plantes** is pure magic. Locals and visitors of all ages—yes, including octogenarians—gather to dance while someone plays music from a portable speaker.

These are informal, welcoming sessions. A charismatic instructor usually leads, teaching salsa, swing, or whatever else emerges. No rhythm? No problem. You can spectate or jump in as a beginner. The vibe is genuinely inclusive.

## 10. Party Beside a Floating Boat Bar on the Seine

Securing a terrace table in **Paris** is notoriously difficult. For a taste of *la belle vie*, book a table at **OFF Paris Seine** or **Noti Club Plage**, floating boat bars that recreate the South of France guinguette experience.

Blue-and-white striped deckchairs, gingham-checked tables, olive trees, and strings of lanterns set the scene. Cocktails, tapas, DJ sets, and dancing follow. The atmosphere is pure Mediterranean fantasy—right in the middle of the **Seine**.

## 11. Stroll the Riverside at Sunset

**Paris** offers countless picturesque riverside walks. The **Île de la Cité** and **Île Saint-Louis** are car-free treasures perfect for evening wandering. The Left Bank is lined with distinctive green-metal stalls selling antique books, vintage French advertising posters, and artwork.

The lesser-known **Île aux Cygnes** features a shaded walkway with stunning **Eiffel Tower** views—the perfect spot for a sunset walk.

## 12. Join Paris Pride

**Paris Pride** brings celebration, visibility, and joy to the streets. The parade and festivities draw massive crowds and represent one of the most vibrant LGBTQ+ celebrations in Europe.
