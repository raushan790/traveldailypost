At nearly 100 degrees, I sat in the cool, stone-walled living room of **Jnane Rumi**, iced mint tea sweating in my palm, a Mediterranean breeze drifting through arched windows. My day had no agenda—just a dog-eared novel, the promise of a poolside dip, and the faint rustle of bougainvillea branches overhead. This is exactly what **Jnane Rumi** delivers: a masterclass in the art of doing absolutely nothing, in absolute style.

Marrakesh already claims some of the world's most celebrated design hotels. **La Mamounia**, a World's Best Awards Hall of Fame winner, has long dominated the luxury landscape. **El Fenn**, recently renovated, continues the tradition of forward-thinking hospitality. But the April 2025 arrival of **Jnane Rumi**—a whisper-quiet, art-filled 12-room guesthouse on the city's outskirts—feels like a seismic shift in Moroccan hospitality. Within months, it was named [one of the best new hotels in the world by Travel + Leisure editors](https://www.travelandleisure.com). This isn't hype. This is earned.

## A Former Private Residence Reimagined

**Jnane Rumi** began life as a private residence, constructed in 1960 by Tunisian architect **Charles Boccara**. For decades, it was home to one family. Then **Gert-Jan van den Bergh**, a Dutch art lawyer, and his wife **Corinne**, a somatic therapist and sculptor, saw something others didn't: potential. They purchased the property, stripped it back to its soul, and rebuilt it as a living gallery.

The couple enlisted **Samy Snoussi**, a Moroccan artist and creative consultant, to curate every piece. What emerged is breathtaking: contemporary North African paintings hang beside European photographs. Frescoes by **Roberto Ruspoli** grace dining room walls. Hand-carved wood ceilings coexist with modern installations. The result feels neither cluttered nor sterile—it feels lived-in, intentional, and joyful.

## The Rooms: 12 Unique Worlds

**Jnane Rumi** offers 12 rooms: seven suites in the main house, four pavilion rooms, and one extraordinary annex with a private pool and garden. Each is radically different. Mine—the **Rabia Annex**—was a riot of color: mint-green doors, vermillion walls, saffron-colored carpets, and a hand-carved wooden canopy bed that looked like it belonged in a sultan's chamber.

The bathroom was appointed with an enormous copper soaking tub and a zellige (traditional Moroccan tilework) shower. A reading nook overlooked gardens planted with fig, olive, and pistachio trees. Books—poetry by **Rumi**, large-format art monographs, novels left by previous guests—lined the shelves. This wasn't decoration. This was life.

Every room features a fireplace, generous seating, and an eclectic mix of antiques, textiles, and contemporary art. No two feel alike. All feel personal.

## Food That Tastes Like Summer

**Karin Gaasterland**, a Dutch chef, oversees a deceptively concise menu that marries Moroccan tradition with French sensibility. Breakfast—included in your rate—arrives as a bountiful spread: cheeses, fruits, yogurt, honey, nuts, croissants, and fresh pastries. Egg dishes can be ordered à la carte.

Lunch is all mezze and poolside simplicity: tabbouleh, tomato salad, feta with olives and grapes, herbed salads, and garlic fries. There's also a fennel and beef burger and a sardine pie if you want something more substantial.

Dinner pivots to tagines. I ordered a whole fish, crowned with spices, oil, and lemon, accompanied by an herby salad of greens and fennel, hummus with a crater of olive oil, and crusty bread. Everything tasted of restraint and freshness—exactly what you crave on a scorching Moroccan evening.

Meals can be served poolside, in the main house dining room, or on your suite's private terrace. The rhythm of the day is yours to set.

## The Art Collection: A Living Museum

Here's what separates **Jnane Rumi** from every other hotel in Marrakesh: the art collection is world-class. These aren't prints or mass-produced prints. These are original works by contemporary North African and European artists. Paintings, photographs, drawings, and frescoes inhabit nearly every room. Walking the property feels like touring a private collector's home—because it essentially is.

This is the hotel's philosophy: beauty and intentionality matter. They're not afterthoughts. They're the entire point.

## Activities: The Power of Stillness

The best thing to do at **Jnane Rumi** is nothing.

After two days of navigating Marrakesh's medinas and souks, I returned to the hotel wanting only silence. I read. I walked among the fruit trees. I napped in the shade by my pool, waking to birdsong. I studied the art. This is radical in our always-on world: permission to be still.

If you do wish to engage with the city, the property can arrange exclusive artist studio visits and private architectural tours. But honestly, you'll fight the urge to leave.

## Spa, Sustainability, and Practicalities

The hotel maintains a small hammam and offers massage treatments in deeply private spaces designed for single guests or couples. The experience is intimate and unhurried.

On sustainability, **Jnane Rumi** maintains organic gardens with water-saving irrigation systems (crucial in the desert), sources seasonal and local ingredients for its restaurant, and partners with local designers and artisans for all furnishings and art.

For accessibility, the main garden, rooftop bar, living room, and dining room are fully accessible. Guests with mobility challenges should book the ground-level Annex or Pavilion rooms rather than the main house, which features stairs but no elevators.

## Location and Booking Details

**Jnane Rumi** sits near the **Palmeraie**—an 11th-century palm oasis—about 15-20 minutes by car from central Marrakesh. The medina, **Yves Saint Laurent Museum**, and **Bahia Palace** are all easily accessible from here.

## The Verdict

**Jnane Rumi** is not a hotel for people who want to maximize their itinerary. It's a hotel for people who want to remember what it feels like to breathe. The art collection is world-class. The food is intelligent. The staff—many of them Moroccan—make you feel instantly at home. The gardens are a sanctuary. And the rooms? They're like staying in someone's remarkably sophisticated private residence.

It was named one of the best new hotels in the world for a reason. You should go before every luxury travel magazine on Earth discovers it.
