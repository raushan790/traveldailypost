Hotels are no longer just places to sleep. **Accor** and the **Fédération de la Haute Couture et de la Mode (FHCM)** just proved it by launching the inaugural **New Crafts Awards**—a bold initiative that transforms luxury hospitality into a creative platform where emerging designers collaborate directly with iconic hotel brands.

This partnership signals a seismic shift in the industry: fashion, design, and cultural experience are now central to how luxury hotels define themselves and attract high-value travellers.

## Peet Dullaert Wins Historic First Award

**Peet Dullaert**, a Dutch couture designer and stylist, has been crowned the first winner of the New Crafts Awards. His winning concept will evolve into a tangible design collaboration with **Swissôtel**, one of Accor's flagship hospitality brands.

The prize isn't just symbolic. Dullaert receives financial support to accelerate his brand development, complimentary stays across the entire Accor hotel portfolio, and direct access to a luxury hospitality ecosystem spanning 100+ countries. This creates a practical pathway where design excellence meets global hotel visibility.

Reddit: *"This is what hospitality should be—not just beds and breakfast, but cultural collaboration and real artist support."* — r/luxury

The jury selection process drew from experts across both Accor and FHCM, ensuring the winning concept authentically reflected **Swissôtel's core values: craftsmanship, absolute quality, and vitalising energy**. The designer brief wasn't arbitrary—it demanded an iconic object or artwork that would become part of the brand's long-term identity.

## Three Finalists Bring International Design Voices

Alongside Dullaert, three finalist designers have secured their own partnership opportunities and ongoing access to Accor's hospitality ecosystem:

- **Egon Lab** — French menswear design house
- **Julie de Libran** — French haute couture designer
- **Kartik Research** — Indian menswear label

This international roster—spanning the Netherlands, France, and India—reflects Accor's commitment to positioning hospitality as a genuine meeting point for global creative voices. Each finalist will participate in brand experiences, cultural activations, and guest-facing creative projects throughout the Accor network.

## An 18-Month Partnership, Not a One-Time Award

Here's where the New Crafts Awards diverge from traditional design competitions: they're structured as an **18-month collaborative engagement**, not a singular recognition event.

Winners and finalists remain embedded within Accor and Swissôtel activities for the duration. They participate in brand experiences, cultural events, and even guest-facing creative installations across the Accor ecosystem. Members of the **ALL Accor loyalty programme** gain exclusive access to fashion shows featuring these award-winning designers—a radical shift from transactional reward points to cultural access.

This isn't just marketing. It's loyalty redefined.

## Paris Fashion Week Anchors the Initiative

The awards are deliberately synchronized with **Paris Fashion Week** and **Haute Couture Week**, embedding participating designers into the world's most influential fashion calendar. This positioning strengthens Accor's cultural footprint within an ecosystem that already drives massive travel demand.

Fashion weeks are economic engines. They attract wealthy international travellers who demand premium accommodation, fine dining, curated retail experiences, and immersive cultural programming. By aligning Swissôtel and the wider Accor portfolio with haute couture, the group is directly capturing this high-value travel segment.

Paris Fashion Week alone generates billions in global travel spending. Accor just positioned its hotels directly into that pipeline.

## The Hospitality Industry Is Transforming

The New Crafts Awards reflect a fundamental evolution in how luxury hotels operate. **Guests no longer expect mere accommodation**—they expect design storytelling, wellness experiences, artistic collaborations, and cultural immersion.

Hotels are evolving into cultural platforms. A Swissôtel object created through this programme will become part of the brand's permanent identity, deepening emotional connection with guests and differentiating the property in an oversaturated luxury market.

This shift addresses a critical industry challenge: how do premium hotels justify price premiums when digital platforms have commodified basic accommodation? The answer: **cultural experience and creative storytelling**.

## Cultural Tourism Drives Destination Economics

The fashion and design traveller demographic is exceptionally high-value. These visitors spend more on accommodation, dining, retail, and cultural experiences than traditional leisure travellers. By integrating emerging designers into hospitality frameworks, Accor is essentially creating new cultural tourism pathways.

Consider the ripple effect: A designer collaboration at Swissôtel Paris attracts fashion-conscious travellers. Those visitors stay longer, book premium rooms, dine at Michelin-starred restaurants, shop at luxury boutiques, and attend gallery openings. A single high-value guest segment multiplies spending across an entire destination economy.

[Research from the United Nations World Tourism Organization](https://www.unwto.org/) confirms that cultural and creative tourism are among the fastest-growing travel segments globally. Accor is betting that luxury hotels will become central nodes in these economies.

## Loyalty Programs Shift From Transactional to Experiential

The ALL Accor programme inclusion signals a broader transformation in hospitality loyalty models. Points and discounts are dead. Exclusive cultural access is the new currency.

Offering privileged access to private fashion previews, designer collaborations, and exclusive creative events turns loyalty programmes into cultural gateways. This approach creates emotional stickiness—guests remain loyal not because of financial incentives, but because of irreplaceable cultural moments they can't access elsewhere.

This differentiation is critical in a market where competitors can easily replicate room amenities and service standards.

## Supporting Emerging Talent Builds Brand Responsibility

Beyond commercial strategy, Accor is investing in creative industry development. By supporting emerging designers, the group positions itself as a facilitator of global creative exchange. This alignment with cultural responsibility and professional growth strengthens brand identity, particularly among affluent, culturally-conscious travellers.

## What This Means for Swissôtel

**Swissôtel** gains an immediate lifestyle upgrade. The brand shifts from luxury accommodation provider to creative platform. The collaboration allows Swissôtel to express its premium positioning through tangible cultural objects and design-led storytelling—a powerful differentiator in the ultra-competitive luxury segment.

## The Bigger Picture: Hospitality as Culture Engine

The New Crafts Awards represent a structural shift in how the hospitality industry operates. As traveller expectations evolve, hotels will increasingly collaborate with fashion, design, music, and art sectors.

Accor's partnership with FHCM demonstrates that such collaborations generate simultaneous value for brands, designers, destinations, and travellers. This isn't zero-sum competition—it's ecosystem expansion.

The lasting impact could extend far beyond branding. Hotels may fundamentally transform into active participants in cultural production, shaping how travellers experience destinations worldwide.

This is the future of luxury hospitality: not passive accommodation, but cultural platforms where design, fashion, and creative excellence meet travel experience.

*The luxury hotel industry just got its biggest creative infusion in a generation.*
