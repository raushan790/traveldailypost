## What to Do If You're Caught in a Flight Cancellation

If your flight gets axed, panic won't help—but a action plan will.

### Stay Connected and Informed

Don't rely on the departure board alone. Enable push notifications on your airline's app. Monitor your email and text messages. Airlines typically notify passengers within minutes of making cancellation decisions. Check the carrier's website for real-time updates and rebooking information.

### Get to Customer Service Fast

If you're at the airport, head directly to the airline's service desk—not the ticketing counter. Service desk staff handle irregular operations and can rebook you immediately. If you're still home, call the airline's customer service line or use their online chat system. Avoid massive phone wait times by using the chat feature.

### Know Your Legal Rights

Understand what you're entitled to. In the **European Union**, passengers receive compensation under [EC Regulation 261/2004](https://ec.europa.eu/transport/themes/passengers/air-rights_en), typically €250–€600 per person depending on flight distance. In the **US**, compensation varies by airline policy—there's no federal requirement, but many carriers offer rebooking on the next available flight or rival carriers.

### Explore Alternative Routes

Ask the airline about the next available flight on their network. If the wait is unacceptable, ask about booking you on a competing carrier's flight at no extra charge (common in irregular operations). Consider ground transportation: trains or buses can be faster than waiting days for a rebooking.

### Document Everything

Keep your cancellation notice, boarding pass, and any receipts for meals or accommodations. These documents support future compensation claims if the cancellation qualifies.
