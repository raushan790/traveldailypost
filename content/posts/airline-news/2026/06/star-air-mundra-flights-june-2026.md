## A Bold New Chapter for Western India's Aviation Map

**Star Air** just made a calculated bet on one of India's most economically pivotal regions. On **June 23, 2026**, the regional carrier officially launches scheduled commercial services to **Mundra, Gujarat**—marking the airline's **32nd destination** and a decisive expansion into the logistics and industrial heartland of western India.

This isn't just another route announcement. Mundra sits at the crossroads of India's trade revolution, home to the nation's largest privately-operated commercial and container port. Star Air's move signals something larger: a serious commitment to connecting India's economic underbelly, not just its tourist hotspots.

## Flight Schedule: What's Actually Taking Off

Here's what you need to know about the inaugural schedule:

| Route | Frequency | Days of Operation |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| Mumbai to Mundra | 5 weekly | Tuesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday, Sunday |
| Mundra to Delhi NCR (Hindon) | 3 weekly | Tuesday, Thursday, Saturday |

Beyond these direct services, Star Air is threading multiple one-stop connections through Mundra—linking cities like **Goa, Surat, Belagavi, Bengaluru, Kolhapur, and Nanded** across its expanding network. This layered approach transforms Mundra from a simple endpoint into a strategic hub within Star Air's regional architecture.

## Why Mundra Matters (And Why Now)

**Mundra isn't a tourist destination.** It's a trade corridor. The Kutch district in Gujarat has exploded as an industrial and logistics hub over the past decade, yet air connectivity remained fragmented. Star Air's entry directly addresses this gap.

Capt. **Simran Singh Tiwana**, CEO of **Star Air**, framed it plainly: "Every new destination added to our network reflects our belief that enhanced connectivity can unlock opportunities and drive inclusive growth." The airline has strategically leaned into India's **UDAN initiative** (Ude Desh ka Aam Nagrik)—the government scheme designed to democratize air travel across underserved regions.

Since launching operations in **2019**, Star Air has positioned itself as the carrier of choice for India's secondary and tertiary cities. Mundra represents the natural evolution of this strategy.

## The Economic Ripple Effect

**Arun Bansal**, CEO of **Adani Airport Holdings Limited**, didn't shy away from the bigger picture: "This development is expected to boost local tourism, trade, and economic growth." The language matters. Air connectivity directly accelerates business investment, reduces logistics costs, and makes it feasible for executives and traders to move seamlessly between Mundra and Mumbai or Delhi.

For travelers, this means cheaper tickets as competition increases, faster turnaround times for business trips, and improved access to what's become western India's fastest-growing economic center.

## Star Air's Broader Expansion Playbook

This Mundra launch is part of a calculated offensive. The airline operates across a fragmented network of regional and underserved destinations—exactly where the UDAN programme has created both opportunity and government support. [The UDAN scheme has already subsidized air connectivity to over 60 airports across India](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx), many of which were previously unreachable by air.

Star Air's focus on affordability and reliability in these markets has made it a credible challenger to legacy carriers in regional aviation. Mundra marks the 32nd destination, but the airline's trajectory suggests this momentum will continue.

## What This Means for Travelers and Traders

If you're moving cargo through Mundra's port, this changes your supply chain. If you're exploring the industrial parks of Kutch or Surat, you've suddenly got an efficient air option. If you're betting on Gujarat's continued economic growth, improved infrastructure always precedes economic acceleration.

The June 23 launch date is firm. Expect Star Air to heavily promote early bookings on routes from **Mumbai** and **Delhi NCR**, with capacity likely tightening as word spreads about this new gateway.
