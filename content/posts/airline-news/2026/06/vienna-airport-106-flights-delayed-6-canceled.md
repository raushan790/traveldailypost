## Your Legal Shield: EU Regulation 261/2004 Protects Your Rights

Here's what many travelers don't realize: **European Union Regulation EC 261/2004** grants you specific protections when flights are delayed or canceled. This isn't optional courtesy—it's law.

Depending on your specific circumstances, you may qualify for:

- **Meals and refreshments** during extended waits of three hours or more
- **Hotel accommodation** if overnight delays force you to stay in Vienna
- **Alternative transportation arrangements** on the next available flight
- **Cash compensation** ranging from €250 to €600 per person, depending on flight distance and circumstances

The catch: eligibility hinges on whether the disruption falls within the airline's control. Extraordinary circumstances—severe weather, air traffic management decisions, security risks—may exempt carriers from compensation obligations. However, standard operational failures typically don't qualify for exemptions.

If your flight was delayed beyond three hours or canceled, document everything: boarding pass, booking confirmation, delay notification, and any expenses incurred. This documentation becomes critical if you pursue compensation claims through [EU261 enforcement agencies](https://www.ec.europa.eu/info/law/better-regulation_en).

## The Ripple Effect: How Vienna's Chaos Spreads Across Europe

**Vienna International Airport** isn't just another regional hub—it's a critical junction in Europe's air transport network. Aircraft completing multiple daily rotations between European cities mean delays here trigger cascading effects hundreds of miles away.

A delayed arrival from **Paris** doesn't just annoy Parisian-bound passengers; it also blocks the aircraft's next rotation to **Berlin**, which delays the subsequent flight to **Warsaw**, and so on. Airlines call this "network disruption," and it's why even relatively contained problems at one airport can paralyze schedules across the continent.

The airport's role connecting Central and Eastern Europe to Western European capitals and Middle Eastern hubs amplifies its importance. Routes to **Dubai** carry business travelers and leisure passengers whose missed connections have economic consequences far beyond inconvenience.
