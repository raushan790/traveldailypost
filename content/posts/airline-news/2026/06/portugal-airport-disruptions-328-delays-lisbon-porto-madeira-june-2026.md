## What Actually Broke? Understanding the Cascade Failure

Aviation isn't simple. Flight delays don't emerge from a single weak point—they cascade from interconnected systems failing simultaneously.

Aviation experts consistently identify multiple culprits behind large-scale disruptions:

- **Air traffic congestion** — European airspace gridlock during peak hours
- **Weather-related limitations** — Wind, rain, or atmospheric conditions affecting operations
- **Aircraft rotation delays** — Planes arriving late, throwing off entire departure schedules
- **Crew scheduling conflicts** — Crew limitations and mandatory rest periods
- **Ground handling bottlenecks** — Baggage, refueling, cleaning, and passenger boarding delays
- **Technical maintenance requirements** — Mandatory inspections grounding aircraft
- **Air traffic management restrictions** — Continent-wide capacity limits across European skies

The interconnected nature of modern aviation means a single delay at one airport can trigger a domino effect across an entire airline's network. One late-arriving aircraft becomes two delayed departures, which becomes four downstream impacts, which ripples across Europe.

[According to the European Commission's air traffic management data](https://www.eurocontrol.org/), summer capacity challenges are becoming increasingly common across European airspace as post-pandemic travel demand exceeds infrastructure capacity.

## What Passengers Can Actually Do (And What Rights They Have)

If you were affected by June 10's disruptions, understanding your rights matters.

Under **European Regulation (EC) No. 261/2004**, passengers facing significant delays or cancellations may be entitled to:

- Meals and refreshments
- Accommodation (in certain circumstances)
- Alternative transport arrangements
- **Compensation ranging from €250 to €600** (depending on flight distance and delay length)

**Critical caveat**: Eligibility depends on whether the disruption resulted from the airline's actions or extraordinary circumstances beyond the airline's control (weather, air traffic control decisions, security threats). Airlines often claim "extraordinary events" to avoid compensation—a strategy that frequently leads to passenger disputes.

Here's what affected travelers should do immediately:

1. **Check your flight status** before heading to the airport
2. **Document everything**—keep receipts for meals, accommodation, alternative transport
3. **Maintain contact with your airline** and request written confirmation of the delay/cancellation
4. **Understand your specific rights** under EU rules or your country's equivalent regulations
5. **Consider filing a compensation claim** if you're eligible (through your airline, or via [flight compensation services](https://www.flightright.com/) if the airline refuses)
