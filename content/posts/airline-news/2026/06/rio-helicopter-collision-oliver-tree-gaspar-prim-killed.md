The morning of **June 14, 2026**, turned tragic over Rio de Janeiro when two helicopters collided in mid-air, killing all six people aboard. Among the victims: **American singer Oliver Tree**, known for hits like "Life Goes On" and "Miss You," and Argentine content creator **Gaspar Prim**, whose YouTube following spanned millions. The collision has become one of Brazil's deadliest helicopter accidents in recent memory—and investigators are racing to understand how two aircraft operating in one of the world's busiest urban helicopter corridors suddenly became one.

## The Collision: When Two Helicopters Met Disaster

At approximately **08:59 local time**, a **Bell 206B JetRanger III** (registration **PP-MAC**) and an **Aérospatiale AS350B2 Écureuil** (registration **PR-DJJ**) collided in the Recreio dos Bandeirantes district of western Rio de Janeiro. The impact was catastrophic. Both aircraft were destroyed instantly. There were no survivors.

One helicopter plunged directly into a **BYD vehicle storage facility**, triggering an inferno that consumed approximately **20 electric vehicles** and scattered wreckage across a wide radius. Video footage circulating on social media showed one helicopter engulfed in flames as it hit the ground—a stark reminder of the violence of the impact.

Emergency services arrived within minutes, but all six occupants were already dead at the scene.

## The Victims: A Global Loss

**Oliver Tree Nickel**, 29, was in Rio de Janeiro as part of his ongoing world tour when he boarded one of the helicopters. The Los Angeles-based musician had built a devoted fanbase with his synth-pop sound and experimental visual style. His death sent shockwaves through the music industry and social media within hours.

**Gaspar Prim**, known online as "**Gaspi**," was a digital content creator with millions of followers across YouTube and social platforms. He was based in Argentina but frequently traveled internationally for collaborations and appearances.

The four other victims were identified as **Lucas Vignale**, **Lucas Brito Chaves**, pilot **Alexandre Souza**, and pilot **Charles Marsillac**.

## Rio de Janeiro: A Crowded Helicopter Highway

Rio de Janeiro's helicopter traffic is relentless. The city's coastal geography, affluent beachfront neighborhoods, and notorious road congestion have created explosive demand for rotorcraft transportation. Business executives, tourists, and private owners routinely use helicopters to bypass gridlock and travel between districts.

This high-traffic environment makes collision avoidance crucial. Yet on this Sunday morning, something went catastrophically wrong.

According to records from the [Aviation Safety Network](https://aviation-safety.net/), both helicopters have been entered into separate fatal midair collision investigations. The incident represents a rare but devastating failure of what should be a well-coordinated aerial environment.

## Investigation Underway: CENIPA Takes the Lead

Brazil's **Aviation Accident Investigation and Prevention Center** (**CENIPA**) immediately dispatched investigators to the scene. The **SERIPA III** regional investigation service, affiliated with Brazil's Air Force, has been tasked with leading the initial inquiry phase.

Investigators are now examining:

- Flight paths and radar data
- Communications records between both helicopters and air traffic control
- Maintenance documentation for both aircraft
- Weather conditions at the time of collision
- Altitude separation protocols
- Why the pilots failed to detect each other

The Bell 206B was 27 years old; the AS350B2 was 14 years old. Both are well-maintained, globally-trusted aircraft used extensively for tourism, business transport, and utility missions. Age alone is unlikely to be the culprit.

## What Comes Next

Preliminary findings are expected within weeks. A complete technical investigation report could take considerably longer—potentially months. The [Brazilian aviation authorities](https://www.anac.gov.br/) have not yet released flight plans, altitude data, or communications transcripts.

This collision raises urgent questions about helicopter traffic management in major urban centers worldwide. If Rio de Janeiro—one of the planet's most experienced helicopter operating environments—can experience a midair collision, no city is immune.

For now, the aviation community watches and waits. A family mourns an artist whose music touched millions. A digital generation grieves a creator taken too soon.
