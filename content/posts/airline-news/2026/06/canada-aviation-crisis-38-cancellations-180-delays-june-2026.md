## What Affected Passengers Must Do Immediately

**Step 1: Check Your Flight Status**—Don't wait for an airport visit. Open the airline's mobile app or website immediately. Check [real-time flight tracking through FlightAware](https://flightaware.com/) for live updates.

**Step 2: Pursue Digital Rebooking**—Bypass the customer service queues at **Toronto Pearson** or **Montreal-Trudeau**. Most carriers now offer app-based rebooking for cancellations and delays. **Air Canada**, **Jazz Aviation**, **PAL Airlines**, and **Air Borealis** all have self-service options.

**Step 3: Document Everything**—Retain boarding passes, receipts for meals, hotel bookings, and any out-of-pocket expenses. Under Canadian air passenger protection regulations, you're entitled to compensation and care depending on disruption cause and duration.

**Step 4: Know Your Rights**—If your delay exceeds the regulatory threshold, you're owed meal vouchers. If an overnight stay is required, the airline must provide hotel accommodation and ground transportation—provided the disruption is within their control.

**Step 5: For Remote Connections**—If you're rerouting through **Goose Bay** or other regional hubs, contact **PAL Airlines** or **Air Borealis** directly. Inter-line agreements may allow rebooking on alternative carriers if direct options evaporate.

## Compensation Claims: The Paper Trail

Submit claims through the airline's official website within the required timeframe (typically 30 days). Include:

- Original boarding pass
- Delay/cancellation confirmation email
- Receipts for accommodations, meals, ground transportation
- Official delay notification from the carrier
- Proof of financial loss (missed meetings, hotel rebooking fees, etc.)

Regional carriers like **Air Borealis** handle claims differently than **Air Canada**. Check your carrier's specific compensation policy before submitting.
