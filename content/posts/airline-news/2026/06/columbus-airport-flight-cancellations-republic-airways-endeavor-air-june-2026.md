## What to Do When Your Flight Gets Cancelled: Your Action Plan

If you're caught in a situation like this, panic helps no one. Here's your playbook:

### Stay Updated Immediately

The moment you receive cancellation notification (via email, SMS, or airline app), acknowledge it. Don't assume you know what's happening next. Airlines update situations minute-by-minute during crises.

### Contact Airline Customer Service — The Right Way

Don't sprint to the airport hoping to sort it out in person. Use the airline's app, website, or phone line first. Customer service queues at the airport during disruptions can stretch 2-3 hours. Digital channels move faster.

### Know Your Rights

Under **EU Regulation 261/2004**, passengers are entitled to compensation for cancellations within the airline's control (up to €600 depending on distance). In North America, compensation policies vary by airline, so check your carrier's specific policy immediately.

### Explore Rebooking Options

Ask for the next available flight — but be specific. If the rebooking adds 8+ hours and you can reach your destination via another carrier, ask about that option instead. Some airlines will book you on competitors during major disruptions.

### Consider Alternative Transportation

For some routes, Amtrak or bus services might actually get you there faster than waiting for the next available flight. A **Columbus to New York City** trip via **Northeast Regional rail** takes 14 hours — sometimes worth considering if the alternative is a 24-hour wait.
