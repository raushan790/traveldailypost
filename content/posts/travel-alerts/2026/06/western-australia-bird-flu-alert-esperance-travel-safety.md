## Essential Visitor Safety Guidelines for Coastal Regions

Authorities are reinforcing five core safety rules for anyone exploring Western Australia's natural areas:

**Avoid direct contact with sick or dead birds.** This is the golden rule. If you spot an injured or deceased bird, report it to local authorities rather than attempting rescue or investigation.

**Stay on marked trails in national parks and nature reserves.** Designated paths protect both visitors and sensitive habitats where seabirds nest and rest.

**Follow all posted signage** in coastal and protected areas. These signs communicate real-time wildlife management information.

**Report unusual wildlife sightings** to tour guides or local ranger stations. Eco-tour operators are trained to respond appropriately.

**Wash hands thoroughly** after outdoor activities, especially before eating or drinking.

This isn't about fear—it's about respect for wildlife and basic infectious disease prevention that applies year-round, regardless of H5N1 presence.

Migratory seabirds travel thousands of kilometers across seasons. They cluster in coastal wetlands, nesting colonies, and feeding zones. When avian flu emerges anywhere in global bird populations, coastal regions become natural surveillance focal points.

**Western Australia's coastline** hosts dozens of seabird species, from little penguins to wedge-tailed eagles. These populations naturally attract researchers, birdwatchers, and eco-tourists. Enhanced monitoring ensures early detection while maintaining tourism access.
